Experimental · Opt-in · Non-custodial · 0% fee

Browser miner. Made for Desktops/Laptops/Chromebooks

A standalone experiment: this page runs RandomX in a Web Worker and sends shares to MoneroOcean under your wallet. We take 0% — no cut of your hashrate, no redirected shares (the pool may charge its own fee). Expect toy H/s. A hidden tab stops hashing.

iPhone / iPad: tap Share, then Add to Home Screen. It opens full-screen with no address bar. Its storage is separate from Safari's, so you paste your wallet once more — and it mines only while it is open in front of you.
This browser on iOS cannot install web apps. Open this page in Safari to add it to the Home Screen.

Before you start

  • You start it. Nothing hashes until you press Start. Closing the tab stops it.
  • Your wallet, your coins. The address goes to the pool as the stratum login. VoltMiner never holds XMR.
  • 0% fee. We take no cut of the hashrate and never swap your payout address. MoneroOcean may apply its own pool fee.
  • Ad-blockers will fight this. uBlock Origin and some antivirus flag Wasm miners even with consent. Pause them on this page or the WebSocket never opens.
  • Desktop and Android first. Fast mode is opt-in. Left off, the tab runs light/cache with a ~16 MB Wasm heap and needs almost no RAM. Turned on, it grows to about 2.5 GB as it builds the dataset, so 8 GB machines may still swap — leave it off if you are short on memory.
  • iPhone / iPad runs, slowly. It installs to the Home Screen and hashes, but iOS refuses the ~2 GB fast-mode dataset, so it stays in light mode — and it only runs while the app is open in front of you. Lock the screen and it stops.
  • It runs as an app, not a background service. Installed, it opens full-screen with no address bar and works offline from its own cache. Closing it stops the hashing; nothing keeps running behind your back.
  • One dataset, extra cores. With Fast mode on (and this page cross-origin isolated) the C++ pthreads share a single ~2 GB dataset — each extra thread adds only its scratchpad. The default is light/cache mode: it starts instantly, but hashes several times slower per share. Wasm: low-hundreds of H/s at best (~250–270 H/s at 4 threads on an M3 MacBook Air), not thousands.
  • Keep the tab visible. Background tabs pause Workers. Do not mash Start — extra logins can get the relay rate-limited.
  • RandomX, JIT-compiled in Wasm. Same algorithm family as native miners (rx/0). A WebAssembly JIT compiles RandomX programs as they are generated — roughly 4× the portable interpreter — but there are no huge pages, so it still falls well short of native.
  • Why a bridge relay. A browser cannot open a raw TCP or TLS stratum socket to the pool directly, so a lightweight WebSocket-to-Stratum bridge forwards messages between the browser and pool without altering credentials, jobs, or shares.

Standard (95) or integrated (106) Monero address. Never a private key.

Relay: — — WebSocket hop to the pool. A bridge is required because browsers cannot open raw TCP stratum sockets. Run node tools/web-stratum-bridge/server.mjs locally, or enter a remote bridge URL. See /transparency.

What this page does — and what it does not

        
This is a consented cryptominer; that is the whole point of the page. Here is the short version,
          and the commands to check it yourself.

        

          

            It does
            
    
              
  • Start hashing only after you press Start, and stop on Stop or when the tab closes.
    • 
              
  • Send shares to MoneroOcean with your address as the pool login — the one you typed.
    • 
              
  • Write one thing to this site's private storage: a ~2 GB RandomX dataset cache, reused so it is not rebuilt every visit.
    • 
              
  • Remember two small things locally: your address, and the VMW-… worker id.
    • 
              
  • Keep a copy of its own files — this page, its script, its stylesheet, its icons — in one on-device cache, so the app opens offline. That cache holds nothing from anyone else.
    • 
              
  • Open a single WebSocket to your configured stratum bridge relay, which forwards bytes to the pool unchanged.
    • 
            

          

          

            It does not
            
    
              
  • Ask for a seed phrase or private key. Ever. Anyone who does is stealing from you.
    • 
              
  • Mine before you press Start, or keep mining once the tab or app is closed.
    • 
              
  • Install a system service or a background agent. "Add to Home Screen" is a shortcut, a full-screen window and a cache of this page — nothing runs when it is shut.
    • 
              
  • Load third-party scripts, fonts, analytics or ads — the CSP header forbids it (default-src 'none').
    • 
              
  • Use cookies, or ask for camera, microphone, location or your files.
    • 
              
  • Touch anyone's coins: mining pays the address you pasted, and no key is ever involved.
    • 
            

          

        

        
Check the bytes you were served yourself:
          curl -s https://j3r0nim0.github.io/voltminer-web/js/vendor/randomx/web-randomx-mt.wasm | shasum -a 256
          — published hashes, the wasm patch, the build recipe and the bridge's source live in
          /transparency. The bridge rewrites nothing, so a redirected payout would show up as
          somebody else's address on the MoneroOcean dashboard.

        
The offline copy is inspectable too: a browser keeps it under
          DevTools → Application → Service Workers and Cache Storage, and a phone keeps it under
          Settings → Safari → Advanced → Website Data. Diagnose the device instead of guessing —
          /diag.html. If you ever want it gone, Delete cached dataset plus
          Forget my wallet plus clearing site data removes every byte this page stored.
